# Face tags in family photos

The existing shared photo viewer now supports face positions. Every uploaded photo opened from the Family Gallery, shared photos, personal albums, weddings, nostalgia, nursery albums, home slideshow or a shared media link uses the same viewer. Thumbnail grids, image dimensions and ordinary tagging controls retain their existing layout.

Detection finds regions containing faces. Names are assigned only by an explicit visitor selection from the existing family-name picker. No identity recognition, face embeddings, age or gender estimates are created. Existing whole-image tags are offered first as choices, never assigned automatically to a face.

Face buttons are invisible until hover, keyboard focus or a tap. Clicking an unlabelled face opens a small search panel beside it, preferring its physical right and clamping to the visible viewport. Only one name/picker can be visible. Desktop names disappear on pointer exit; phone names disappear after three seconds. A named face offers an edit action on touch. Clicking a missed face creates a manual region, which can be adjusted by dragging. The viewer offers 100–800% zoom, scrolling and a fit-photo reset. Face coordinates stay normalized against the original image at every zoom level; a drag scroll cannot create a tag. Manual boxes start at 32 displayed pixels and shrink in original-image coordinates as the user zooms in. Actual face containment takes priority over a nearby expanded touch target, and a broad box cannot swallow smaller independent faces through containment-only deduplication. The normal photo scroll and touch behavior remains active outside explicit adjustment mode.

A coarse detection that spans two independent, supported smaller faces is filtered before publication and again when choosing a face. This prevents a false box around two adjacent back-row heads from capturing both selections. Existing saved face labels are retained. The regression uses the actual normalized regions from the Tomer and Shani wedding photo and checks each selection at phone and enlarged display sizes.

Detection runs in an image-only Web Worker on demand, not across thumbnail grids. Source bitmaps retain up to a 4096-pixel long edge within a 12-megapixel budget, without upscaling low-resolution originals. The primary YuNet 2023mar detector runs with ONNX Runtime Web 1.22.0 in single-threaded CPU/WASM, including browsers without WebGL. Its 640-pixel model input receives the whole photo plus overlapping 1024/384-pixel crops. Fine crops magnify tiny background faces without inventing pixels. Crops containing candidates also receive a horizontally mirrored pass, with eye/mouth landmark ordering mapped back correctly. Scores below .65 require at least two independent views and a score of .48 or above. Landmark-aware suppression keeps overlapping people separate while consolidating repeated predictions. An eight-model-pixel guard at internal crop seams rejects false face slivers; genuine photograph edges remain eligible. Landmark coordinates are ephemeral, stay in the detection worker and are not saved or used for identity matching. MediaPipe Tasks Vision 0.10.35 uses the short-range BlazeFace model with a whole-photo pass and square 1152/512-pixel crops, at least 25% overlap and full edge coverage. Cut-off detections at internal crop seams are discarded in favour of intact boxes from overlapping crops. The model's suppression threshold is .45 to retain nearby overlapping faces. A local PICO classifier scans upright photos at up to 2560 pixels and the other three orientations at up to 1920 pixels with a 16-pixel minimum scan window and .35 clustering IoU. It supplements successful model detections and remains a fallback if both neural detectors are unavailable. YuNet does not require the graphics support needed by MediaPipe. Stronger PICO scores are required for supplemental detections. The displayed photo remains unchanged. If automatic detection is unavailable, manual tagging still works. Partly occluded and very small faces are now scanned by the detailed model as well; fully hidden faces or originals lacking sufficient pixels can still require manual selection.

The worker accepts network requests only to this site, plus a GET for the exact public MediaPipe runtime URL pinned by SHA-256 integrity. That runtime request omits credentials and referrer information. Photos and detector input pixels are never sent to an external recognition API. YuNet, its WASM runtime, the MediaPipe model and the PICO fallback are served as static assets. YuNet adds approximately 11.5 MB of lazily loaded, cacheable runtime/model assets only when face detection is requested. The worker's separate CSP also limits connections. Third-party source, versions, hashes and licences are recorded under `public/assets/vendor/`.

`GET /api/media/<id>/faces` returns small face metadata for one photo. `POST /api/media/<id>/faces/save` creates, updates or clears one face association, using an expected face revision to protect concurrent edits. Rectangles are normalized to the original image, validated and limited to 150 per photo. The minimum side is .0005 of the original dimension, allowing precise storage of distant faces smaller than the previous .005 limit. Overlap alone does not reject a second person: duplicate-save checks also require near-identical box centres. Genuine duplicates still reject atomically. A label and its ordinary photo tag are written in one transaction. Face edits do not remove unrelated ordinary tags or affect another photo. Clearing a face label preserves the ordinary tag. Ordinary untagging hides the matching face labels; undo restores them. Soft deletion and restoration retain face metadata. Exports include `faceTags` alongside the original media records.

The client keys metadata and detection caches by media ID, cancels abandoned viewer work, avoids re-running detection for recently viewed photos, and reconciles face saves with an ordinary tag request that is already running. Early detections are displayed while finer scans continue, with stable IDs for repeated boxes. Closing or switching photos terminates an active scan, including safe handling of a bitmap decode that finishes after cancellation. A 90-second worker deadline preserves any useful early results. Coordinates are projected against the displayed image after resizing, including phone and keyboard-constrained viewports.

## Partially tagged gallery

The Family Gallery offers `all`, `untagged`, and `partial` filters over the same media records. `partial` includes an indexed photo when its detected/positioned face count exceeds its ordinary name-tag count and there is at least one unlabelled region. Zero-tag photos can belong to both `untagged` and `partial`; every photo stays in `all`. Labelling every region also completes a photo when the same person appears more than once. Old whole-photo tags count toward classification, but do not establish which face belongs to a name. The picker offers those existing names for explicit face assignment; no identities are inferred.

Only photos opened from the `partial` gallery receive permanent gold dashed outlines and question badges on unlabelled regions. Saving a face association removes its outline immediately. The collection, counters, search and all pagination pages update automatically. The active photo stays open while its finished thumbnail disappears from the filtered collection. Other galleries retain their existing invisible-until-interaction controls.

`gallery-faces.js` scans the entire gallery progressively, one original photo at a time, while the gallery is visible. The queue is independent of the selected page, search and filter. Opening a viewer or hiding/leaving the gallery cancels background work; a viewer scan takes priority. Each completed scan is stored once in `media_face_scans`, with normalized rectangles, detector version and the photo version. Results are shared across visitors; failed or incomplete scans are not recorded as zero-face photos, and failures can be retried. Unknown photos are not assumed complete. The status under the filters shows remaining work. Concurrent results are consolidated instead of allowing a shorter result to erase known regions. Scan metadata is included in exports; edited/restored photo versions invalidate it for a fresh scan, and soft deletion preserves it for undo.

`GET /api/gallery/face-scan?after=<rowid>` provides a bounded cursor batch of unindexed photos and the pending count. `POST /api/media/<id>/faces/detection` validates `{photoRevision, version, faces}` against the current source. `GET /api/media/<id>/faces` also returns saved detection regions, so reopening a photo can draw markers immediately. Saved face labels and ordinary tag changes update the indexed coverage in the same database transaction. Completed scans invalidate collection ETags; different filters have separate cache keys. Timeout/worker-error partial results remain useful in the viewer but are not cached as complete or published as a completed gallery index.

Gallery indexing and its visible missing-face markers use the completed YuNet/MediaPipe evidence before PICO supplementation. PICO-only suggestions remain available in normal manual tagging, but do not independently classify a photo as incomplete. This avoids counting ornaments and texture detections as missing people. If no neural detector completes, the photo remains unindexed and retryable; it is never recorded as a completed zero-face scan. `galleryFaces` carries this separate evidence through the detector cache, and scan version 2 refreshes earlier indexes.
